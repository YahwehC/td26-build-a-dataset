Build Your Own Dataset
TAMU Datathon | Participant Challenge Guide
Great software starts with a good question. For this challenge, your team must leave the screen, collect original data from the real world during TAMU Datathon, and turn that data into a usable, interactive application.
The Challenge
Find something worth observing, measuring, counting, photographing, recording, or asking people about. Collect and document a dataset yourselves, then use it as an essential part of an application people can actually interact with. The collection process should be genuine, and the data should drive a meaningful feature, not merely appear in a screenshot or decorative chart.
There is no required dataset size or prescribed tech stack. A smaller, carefully collected dataset powering a thoughtful application can be stronger than a huge, low-quality one.
Rules & Eligibility
1. Collect new primary data during the hackathon. The primary dataset must be gathered by your team during the event, not recycled from earlier projects.
2. Engage with the physical world. Your observations can come from places, objects, environmental conditions, movement, sounds, images, or consenting participants. Use lawful, safe collection methods.
3. Build a functioning, interactive application. Websites, mobile apps, games, maps, and interactive tools qualify. A static report, standalone notebook, dashboard screenshot, or trained model without a usable interface does not.
4. Make your own data central. Public datasets, APIs, and web scraping may supplement your work, but they cannot supply the main dataset or substitute for real-world collection.
5. Document how you gathered the data. Judges should be able to understand your collection location or context, method, timing, sample size, fields, processing, and limitations.
6. Publish your work. Include the dataset, collection methodology, source code, and instructions to run the app in a GitHub repository. Do not publish private or sensitive information; use anonymized or non-sensitive data instead.
What Could You Build?
• Campus accessibility explorer: Survey routes, ramps, doors, and obstacles, then build an interactive campus map that helps users compare accessible paths.
• Noise or study-space finder: Take time-stamped sound readings in permitted public spaces, then build a searchable tool for finding quieter study spots.
• Foot-traffic insights: Count pedestrian activity at multiple public locations and times, then build an interactive crowd-pattern or route-planning tool.
• Local biodiversity spotter: Record plant or animal sightings without disturbing habitats, then build an identification, mapping, or exploration app.
• Campus wait-time tracker: Observe queues at permitted locations, then build an app that visualizes patterns and lets users explore likely busy periods.
• Community preference explorer: Conduct an optional, consent-based survey without collecting identifying details, then build an interactive comparison or recommendation tool.
These are starting points, not restrictions. Original ideas and inventive ways of collecting data are encouraged.
What Does Not Qualify?
• Downloading a Kaggle dataset and building an app around it.
• Primarily scraping websites or calling third-party APIs, with little or no new physical-world data collection.
• Reusing data collected before the hackathon as your primary dataset.
• Submitting only a written analysis, static visualization, or ML model without an interactive application.
• Fabricating observations, presenting generated data as real measurements, or misrepresenting how data was collected.
Required Devpost Submission
Submit both of the following through your TAMU Datathon Devpost project before the event's submission deadline:
1. GitHub repository link: Include your application source code, newly collected dataset (in a safe, shareable format), README with setup/run instructions, collection methodology, data dictionary or field descriptions, and a brief discussion of limitations. Clearly label any supplementary public or synthetic data.
2. Two-minute demonstration video: Show (a) how and where your team collected real-world data, (b) what the dataset contains, and (c) the working application in action, including how its features use the collected data. Aim for approximately 2 minutes; keep the video focused and accessible to judges.
A polished deployment is optional unless otherwise required by general event rules, but judges must be able to understand and evaluate how the application works through your repository and video.
Judging Rubric (100 Points)
Data Collection & Quality — 20 points. Originality and rigor of the collection process, sensible sampling, accurate records, and clear documentation.
Application Functionality & Data Integration — 20 points. Usable interactive experience, reliable behavior, and meaningful dependence on the collected dataset.
Creativity & Originality — 20 points. Inventiveness of the question, collection method, solution, and user experience.
Usefulness & Impact — 20 points. Relevance to a real need or interesting insight, practical value, and thoughtful treatment of data limitations.
Communication & Presentation — 20 points. Clarity of the video, explanation of the collection process and app, and understandable repository documentation.
Judges will assess quality and execution, not simply dataset size, complexity of the technology, or production polish.
Collect Data Responsibly
• Respect privacy and consent. Ask permission before interviewing, surveying, photographing, or recording identifiable people. Avoid collecting personal or sensitive information unless strictly necessary and appropriately consented to; the safer default is not to collect it.
• Respect property and venue rules. Collect only where permitted; do not enter restricted spaces or interfere with staff, customers, classes, traffic, or other events.
• Stay safe. Do not collect data in roads, hazardous areas, or situations that place anyone at risk. Never disturb wildlife or damage property.
• Be transparent. Explain collection methods honestly; do not fabricate, secretly record, or mislabel data. Clearly distinguish measured, estimated, simulated, and externally sourced values.
• Share responsibly. Remove identifying details, precise sensitive locations, credentials, and other restricted information before publishing the repository or video. If raw data cannot be safely published, collect a safe dataset or provide a properly anonymized version.
Before You Submit
□ We gathered our primary dataset ourselves during TAMU Datathon.
□ Our application is functional, interactive, and meaningfully uses our dataset.
□ Our GitHub repository contains the shareable dataset, methodology, code, and setup instructions.
□ Our Devpost submission includes the GitHub link and a roughly 2-minute video of collection and application demo.
□ We followed applicable venue rules, respected consent, and removed sensitive information.
Go outside. Notice something interesting. Measure it. Build something that would not exist without your data.
