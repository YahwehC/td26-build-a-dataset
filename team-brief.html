MSC Field Brief: what we collect and who does it

We are building an interactive map of the Memorial Student Center that answers a practical question: where should I go right now? It covers study spots, water bottle fillers and restrooms. The Aggie Map shows where buildings are. Our map shows which spot is quiet, which filler works, and which restroom has soap, using numbers we measure ourselves this weekend.

Why we have to go out and measure

The challenge requires that the main dataset is collected by our team during the event, in the physical world. The app must actually depend on that data. Judges score collection quality, app and data integration, creativity, usefulness and presentation (20 points each). Careful, honest records beat a big messy dataset.

Never make up a value. If you didn't measure it, leave it blank and add a note. Fabricated data disqualifies the project.

The plan in one minute

  1. Round 0 (inventory, about 60 min): each person walks their zone and logs every study spot, water filler and restroom, with its fixed details.
  2. Rounds 1–4 (about 15 min each): walk the same route again and log only the things that change: how many people, how loud, the filler counter number.
  3. Between rounds, keep working on other challenges. Total effort is about 2 hours per person across the weekend.

Our only female teammate covers the women's restrooms. Restroom details are only logged in R0 and R4, so this is two walks, not five. To keep her total load equal, she gets the smallest zone. All-gender and family restrooms can be logged by anyone. With only 3 people, Zones 1 and 2 merge.

Quality check: in each round, also count one study spot from your neighbor's zone (Zone 1 checks one of Zone 2's spots, 2 checks 3, 3 checks 4, 4 checks 1). Two independent counts of the same spot tell judges how reliable our counts are.

What to record

Every record automatically gets the time, your initials, the round and the zone. Each place gets an ID when you first log it: S = study spot, W = water, R = restroom, A = other amenity, then zone and number, e.g. S-Z2-03. Reuse the same ID in every later round.

Study spot every round

A study spot is any public seating area where someone could sit and work: a lounge, a group of tables, a row of counter seats. Not meeting rooms with a closed door and not restaurant seating behind a counter.

How to record it

A phone form is coming: you open a link, pick a place, tap in the numbers, and it stamps the time and saves on your phone even without signal. At the end of each round you tap Export and send the file to the team chat.

Until the link is ready, type one line per place in your Notes app in exactly this format:

R1 | S-Z2-03 | 18:04 | people 7 | taken 6/20 | 52 dB | note: none
R1 | W-Z2-01 | 18:09 | working y | green | counter 48213

Rules while collecting

  • Do not photograph or film people's faces. Film fixtures, the counter display, your phone screen and your hands. We need a few short clips of collection for the 2-minute demo video, so take some.
  • Stay in public areas. Don't enter closed rooms, staff areas or events in progress, and don't block anyone.
  • If someone asks what you are doing, tell them: "We're mapping study spots and water fillers in the MSC for TAMU Datathon. We only count seats, we don't record anyone."
  • Record on the spot, not from memory afterwards.
  • Same route order every round, so each spot is measured at about the same offset from the round start.
  • Unsure? Write a note instead of guessing.

Before R0, bring

  • Charged phone with the sound meter app installed and tested
